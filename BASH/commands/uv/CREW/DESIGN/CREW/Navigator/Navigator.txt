Navigator
In the Traveller universe, the Navigator (sometimes called the Astrogator) is the ship’s resident mathematician and cosmic cartographer. While the Pilot handles the "stick" in real-space, the Navigator is responsible for the ship's position in the fourth dimension.

Without a skilled Navigator, a ship is just a very expensive coffin waiting to vanish into the "Blind Eternities" of Jump space.
1. Jump Plotting (The Prime Responsibility)

The Navigator’s most critical duty is calculating the Jump Profile. This isn't just pointing the ship at a star; it’s a high-stakes calculus problem involving gravity wells, stellar drift, and the ship’s specific drive performance.

    Plotting the Course: Using the Astrogatorm skill to calculate the jump. A failed roll can lead to a Misjump, which might dump the ship in empty space, destroy the drive, or emerge months later than planned.

    Gravity Well Avoidance: Ensuring the exit and entry points are outside the "100-diameter" limit of planets and stars. Jumping too close to a world is the fastest way to trigger a catastrophic drive failure.

    The "Jump-6" Challenge: The further the jump, the more variables there are. Plotting a Jump-1 is routine; plotting a Jump-6 is a masterwork of spatial computation.

2. Sensor Operations and "Lookout"

In deep space, you can't see "up" or "down." The Navigator translates raw data into a tactical map.

    Passive & Active Scans: Managing the ship’s sensors to detect incoming vessels, navigation hazards (like asteroid belts), or hidden pirate caches.

    Signature Analysis: Identifying a ship’s type and origin by its heat and radiation signature before it gets close enough to fire.

    Electronic Counter-Countermeasures (ECCM): Cutting through enemy jamming to keep a lock on a target or a destination.

3. Real-Space Navigation and Orbits

Once the ship exits Jump, the Navigator’s job transitions to orbital mechanics.

    Orbital Insertion: Calculating the precise burn needed to enter a stable orbit around a planet or moon.

    Approach Vectors: Coordinating with the Pilot to find the most efficient path to a starport, often while dodging orbital debris or naval blockades.

    Time-to-Target: Managing the "Intercept" math. If the ship needs to catch a fleeing freighter, the Navigator tells the Engineer how much thrust is required and for how long.

4. Library Data and Cartography

The Navigator is the keeper of the ship’s Library Data.
Duty	Description
Chart Updates	Downloading the latest star charts at Class A/B starports to account for stellar movement.
Surveying	In "Scout" campaigns, the Navigator records data on uncharted worlds, which can be sold for significant credits.
Legal Clearances	Ensuring the ship has the correct "navigational beacons" and codes for restricted Imperial star systems.
5. Communications and Signal Logic

Because they spend so much time in the sensor and computer suites, Navigators often handle the ship's long-range comms.

    Tight-Beam Comms: Aiming high-precision lasers at distant satellites or ships to transmit data without being intercepted.

    Signal Encryption: Ensuring the ship’s logs and transmissions are scrambled to prevent corporate or imperial espionage.

The Navigator’s Skillset

A great Navigator is usually the smartest person on the bridge, relying on:

    Astrogation: The core skill for Jump-plotting.

    Electronics (Sensors/Comms): For "seeing" and "talking" in the void.

    Science (Astronomy/Physics): For understanding the celestial bodies they are navigating around.

    The Navigator’s Nightmare: "The Empty Hex." When a ship misjumps into a hex on the subsector map with no star system, it is up to the Navigator to find a way to get the ship back to civilization before the life support runs out.
