Doctor
In the Traveller universe, the Doctor (or Ship’s Medical Officer) is the thin line between a successful payday and a "Total Party Kill." Because space travel is inherently hostile to organic life—involving high-G maneuvers, radiation, exotic alien pathogens, and high-velocity gauss rounds—the Doctor’s role is both clinical and high-stakes.

Here is a breakdown of the duties and responsibilities for a Traveller Doctor.
1. Trauma and Combat Medicine

When a boarding action goes south or a planetary exploration meets hostile wildlife, the Doctor is the most important person on the team.

    Stabilization: Using the Medical skill to prevent a wounded crewmate from dying (reaching "0" in three physical characteristics).

    Surgery: Performing invasive procedures to repair internal damage caused by combat or accidents.

    Triage: Deciding who gets the limited supply of "Fast Drug" (cryo-sleep in a pill) to suspend their metabolism until the ship reaches a high-tech starport.

2. Low Berth Management

Many ships carry "Low Passengers" in cryo-sleep (Frozen Watch). This is statistically the most dangerous way to travel.

    Revival Oversight: Every time a passenger is thawed, a Medical check is required. If the Doctor fails, the passenger dies.

    Maintenance: Ensuring the "Ice Boxes" don't malfunction during the week-long jump.

    Emergency Thawing: Knowing how to safely wake the "Frozen Watch" (backup crew) if the main crew is incapacitated.

3. Xenobiology and Epidemiology

Stepping off a ship onto a new world means exposing the crew to billions of years of alien evolution.

    Bio-Hazard Screening: Running scans to ensure the crew isn't bringing "space plague" back onto the ship.

    Decontamination: Managing the airlocks and ship’s scrubbers if a biological threat is detected.

    Alien Physiology: Understanding how to treat non-human sophonts, like the Vargr, Aslan, or Hivers, whose biologies react differently to human medicine.

4. Long-Term Health and Life Support

The Doctor works closely with the Chief Engineer to ensure the ship's environment doesn't slowly kill the inhabitants.
Responsibility	Description
Radiation Treatment	Monitoring "rad" intake after solar flares or reactor leaks and administering anti-rad drugs.
Psychological Health	Managing "Jump Cabin Fever." A week in a metal box can lead to psychosis or depression.
Pharmacology	Managing the ship's supply of "Medical Slow," "Combat Drugs," and "Anagathics" (anti-aging drugs).
Cybernetic Maintenance	Overseeing the integration and repair of neural links or prosthetic limbs.
5. Medical Admin and Law

Medical care in the Imperium is highly regulated.

    Death Certificates: Legally certifying deaths in deep space for Imperial records.

    Malpractice Avoidance: Maintaining the ship’s Sickbay to "High Guard" standards to avoid lawsuits at the next Class A Starport.

    Forensics: Using medical knowledge to investigate suspicious deaths—vital in many "murder mystery" Traveller scenarios.

The Doctor’s Arsenal

A Doctor’s effectiveness is determined by their Medical skill level and the Tech Level (TL) of their equipment:

    Medkit: Portable trauma gear for the field.

    Sickbay: The dedicated room on a ship (usually required for ships over 200 tons) that provides bonuses to healing.

    Automed: A robotic surgical table that can perform basic tasks if the Doctor is the one who is injured.

    The Moral Dilemma: In Traveller, anagathic drugs (which stop aging) are highly illegal on some worlds and incredibly expensive everywhere. A Doctor often has to decide if they are willing to participate in the "Grey Market" to keep wealthy patrons—or themselves—young.
